# Protests over Mykhailo Fedorov leaving the Defence Ministry — media collection

**Snapshot: 6 August 2026.** The protest is in its **twenty-second consecutive day (16 July – 6 August) and still ongoing**, so **figures remain preliminary**. Each city carries the **peak over the 22-day wave**, not the count at any single hour, and now also carries how long it lasted: `first_day`, `last_day`, `days_active` and `peak_day`. Where a crowd was still growing when last recorded, its figure is a lower bound; every figure is therefore given with the time it is valid for. The days 5–22 rows added in this run are tagged `run = 6 Aug`; earlier rows keep their original run tags (`13:00`, `15:00`, `18 Jul`, `19 Jul`, `19 Jul PM`) unchanged.

> The tables below are **generated** from `cities.csv` and `publications.csv` by `build_report.py`. Edit the CSVs, not this file. `check_consistency.py` verifies that the artifacts agree.

> **Quotes stay in the original Ukrainian.** `quote_uk` is defined as the outlet's exact words; translating a quote turns evidence into paraphrase, which is the failure mode documented in warning 3. English glosses live in `quote_en`.

## CONTEXT

**⚠️ Updated on 6 August (days 5–22 added).** The previous snapshot closed on 19 July with the wave in its fourth day and the note that "further days are likely". They were: the protests have not stopped since, and this run extends the dataset by eighteen days. The 15:00 snapshot on 16 July recorded that no candidacy for defence minister had been submitted and the ministry sat empty; it also carried an earlier error, since corrected, that the president had already submitted Ihor Klymenko's candidacy. Both were overtaken by events: on 17 July the Cabinet named an **acting** minister — not Klymenko but Yevhen Khmara.

What happened:

- **14.07.2026** — parliament accepted the resignation of Yuliia Svyrydenko's government. The entire cabinet, Fedorov included, became acting ministers.
- **15.07.2026** — Fedorov confirmed he was leaving the post, which he had held since 14.01.2026.
- **16.07.2026, from 09:00** — «cardboard protests» in {N} cities. The Kyiv action was called by veteran **Dmytro Koziatynskyi**, one of the organisers of the July 2025 protests in defence of NABU and SAPO.
- **16.07.2026, 12:12** — parliament elected **Serhii Koretskyi** prime minister, «За — **289**» (Yaroslav Zhelezniak; confirmed by LB at 12:13). The Defence and Foreign ministries were **deliberately excluded** from the cabinet vote.
- **17.07.2026** — protests ran for a **second day**. In Kyiv the evening crowd on Ivan Franko Square reached **«близько 3 тисяч людей»** (LB, own correspondent) and, by several accounts, **exceeded the first day** (Kyiv24: «зібрала значно більше людей, ніж у перший день акції»). Demands widened: besides Fedorov's return, protesters now called for the **dismissal of Commander-in-Chief Oleksandr Syrskyi** and the **recall of MPs** from a month-long recess. Zelensky publicly confirmed for the first time a **conflict between Syrskyi and Fedorov**, calling it a problem of national scale.
- **17.07.2026, 17:39** — the Cabinet, in agreement with the President, named **Yevhen Khmara acting Minister of Defence** and Andrii Sybiha acting Foreign Minister (LB). Fedorov had **declined** Zelensky's offer of an adviser post.
- **18.07.2026** — protests ran for a **third day**. The Kyiv action on Ivan Franko Square ran from ~15:00 and reached **«близько 2 тисяч людей»** by 21:45 (Suspilne, now reporting Kyiv directly), below the day-2 peak. **Mykolaiv grew to «понад 150 людей»** (from 45 on day 1, crossing into 100–999) and Kharkiv police counted **«близько 400»** at 20:00 — both wave highs for their cities. Solidarity rallies were also held **abroad** — Warsaw, Brussels, Sydney — outside the map's scope. Late on 18 July Zelensky held long talks with **Fedorov and Syrskyi** and announced a «рішення щодо армії» (a decision on the army) without reversing the reshuffle; Yevhen Khmara (acting SBU head) continues as acting Defence Minister. A Verkhovna Rada petition to keep Fedorov (author Svitlana Sirko, opened 16 July) is gathering signatures.
- **19.07.2026** — protests ran for a **fourth day** and drew the **largest crowd yet in Kyiv: «понад 5000 людей»** on Ivan Franko Square (Interfax-Ukraine via LIGA), above the day-2 peak. Actions continued in ~15 cities — Lviv, Dnipro, Kharkiv («понад 400»), Odesa, Mykolaiv, Ivano-Frankivsk («приблизно 250») and others — and reached **two cities new to the map, Mukachevo and Kamianske**. The Verkhovna Rada **went into recess until 18 August without appointing a defence minister** — Klymenko's candidacy was never submitted (Ivan Vyhivsky took the interior ministry) and **Yevhen Khmara remains acting**. The demand — Fedorov back, Syrskyi out — is unmet, so the protests continue.

- **20–23.07.2026** — the wave settled into a **daily evening routine** and shrank sharply from its opening days. Kyiv fell to «сотні киян» on 20 July and to **«близько 500 людей»** on 22 and 23 July, a figure four separate newsrooms carried identically (Suspilne, Ukrainska Pravda, Hromadske, Korrespondent). Three towns entered the record here, all of them missed by the first snapshot: **Izmail** (Suspilne Odesa states its rallies had run «з 16 липня», so from day 1), **Kalush** («понад 50 людей другий день поспіль», Suspilne Ivano-Frankivsk) and **Sheptytskyi** (the local outlet Raion.Sheptytskyi, third day running). On 23 July protesters added a demand against the appointment of **Vitalii Kim** to the Ministry of Veterans.
- **24.07.2026** — the deadline the protesters had set Zelensky for personnel decisions passed unmet, and organiser Koziatynskyi declared an **open-ended protest** («безстрокові протести») on Ivan Franko Square. Kyiv «близько 500 людей».
- **26–30.07.2026** — the **trough of the wave**. DW headlined «"Картонкові протести" в Києві: учасників стає дедалі менше»; NV ran an explainer on why the actions were fading. Kyiv fell to «близько 150 осіб» on 26 July; Mykolaiv's dialogue-police counts slid from 55 to 25; on 28 July Koziatynskyi called off the Kyiv action because of a missile threat and announced a march instead of the daily rally. **Syrskyi was removed** in this window — by 28 July Suspilne Kharkiv was quoting protesters as «Сирського прибрали, то чому Федоров досі не на своєму місці» — and **Drapatyi** was appointed.
- **31.07.2026** — the **peak of the entire wave**. The «Єдина протестна хода» marched from Shevchenko Park to Ivan Franko Square; Ukrinform and Fakty ICTV put Kyiv at **«близько 6 тисяч осіб»**, which Bukvy attributes to the **police** — the only official crowd estimate the wave ever produced, in any city, on any day. Koziatynskyi called it the most massive protest since the Revolution of Dignity and said roughly **30 cities** marched or rallied the same day; Censor.NET rendered it as the largest in twelve years, Kyiv Post as «десятки тисяч». **Zelensky removed Fedorov from the National Security and Defence Council that same day.** Lviv, Dnipro, Odesa, Chernivtsi, Rivne, Ternopil, Poltava, Kharkiv, Uzhhorod and Vinnytsia all held their own marches.
- **01–06.08.2026** — after the march the numbers collapsed and did not recover: Kyiv «понад 100 осіб» on 1 August, «близько 200 мітингувальників» on 3 August (DW), «близько ста двадцяти людей» on 4 August. Mykolaiv reached **twelve people** on 3 August, Uzhhorod twelve on 5 August. Dnipro changed format to a **silent protest** («тиха акція», «без кричалок») and counted its 23rd local action by 6 August. **Umerov and Klymenko were given new posts on 3 August**; the Office of the President was still answering questions on 5 August about whether Fedorov would return. On **6 August the protests entered their 22nd consecutive day.**

**The finding of this run: the wave did not fade, it spiked and then thinned — and the reshuffle still stands.** Fedorov has not been reappointed and was removed from the NSDC on the day of the largest protest; Syrskyi, whose dismissal the protesters demanded from day 2, **was** removed, and Drapatyi appointed. The movement's peak came on **day 16, not day 1** — the reverse of the usual decay — and its participants have now stood for **22 consecutive days**, in some cities every single one of them. The map records **where** and **how large**, over 16 July – 6 August; it does not adjudicate the political outcome.

## ⚠️ METHODOLOGICAL WARNINGS

**1. Suspilne's national round-up is a stale mirror of its own regional desks.** The text matches word for word; only the figure differs. As of 15:00 the round-up (updated 11:41) still showed Mykolaiv «26 людей» (regional desk: «45», 11:02) and Dnipro «близько 100» (regional desk: «близько 300», 15:00). **No figure from the round-up can be used without checking the regional desk.**

**2. Kharkiv inverts that pattern.** There the round-up gives more («щонайменше 300», unattributed) than the regional desk («близько 200», police spokesperson, 12:10). The direction is opposite to Mykolaiv and Dnipro, so "the regional desk is always higher" fails as a rule. Time resolves it: MediaPort on the scene watched the crowd grow from «близько 100» to «орієнтовно до 300» within half an hour, matching «щонайменше 300». The police figure of «близько 200» at 12:10 is a later snapshot, after part of the crowd had left. **The daily peak is about 300.**

**3. Search snippets fabricate figures.** Phrases recorded that **appear in no page body**: «кілька тисяч» (Kyiv), «близько тисячі» (Kharkiv), «близько 400 молодих людей» (Lviv). Snippets also credited an AFP quote to Reuters, and a Kyiv Post quote to AP. **Every figure here comes from the body of a page that was actually retrieved.**

**4. Headlines inflate.** TSN: headline «вийшли **тисячі людей**», body text «сотні» at most. Gal-Info: «**масштабна** акція», no figure anywhere in the body.

**5. Most "contradictions" are crowd growth, not conflicting sources.** Dnipro 100→300, Cherkasy 30→100, Khmelnytskyi 50→100, Kharkiv 100→300 within half an hour. Every figure is therefore given **with its time**.

**6. `dateModified` is not evidence of an update.** Ternopil, Zaporizhzhia and Chernivtsi carry fresh modification stamps (15:07–15:30) over text that is word-for-word the morning version: the CMS touched the page without a recount. Trust only markers inside the text, such as «ОНОВЛЕНО. 13:30».

**7. Telegram serves timestamps in UTC.** Kyiv is UTC+3. Zhelezniak's posts need +3 (anchored: «289» at 09:12 UTC = 12:13 Kyiv in LB). Carrying his timestamps over as Kyiv time shifts the chronology by three hours.

**8. Ukrainian inflects city names, so a nominative-case grep returns a false zero.** Searching «Кременчук» in KP.UA's text returns nothing, because the text reads «Кременчу**ці**». Same class of trap as encoding: **dumskaya.net and monitor.cn.ua serve windows-1251**, and reading them as UTF-8 also returns a false zero.

**9. An error in a source, left uncorrected:** Censor.NET's Mykolaiv section says Fedorov was dismissed «з посади **міністра цифрової трансформації**». This contradicts the rest of its own text and every other source. Recorded as published.

**10. The figure selection rule.** Take the figure that agrees across several independent sources, is the latest in time and the largest: the **daily peak**, not the count at a given hour. The aim is to estimate how many people turned out at maximum over the whole day. This follows from warning 5: crowds grew, so an early figure is not a smaller estimate but a different moment.

**11. The 19 July run (day 3) refined the «10 тис.» figure and reran discovery.** The day-2 evening «понад 10 тис.» in Kyiv is **not** snippet fabrication after all: Interfax-Ukraine reported it as a wire story (17 Jul, 18:28; retrieved here via Espreso's citation «за даними „Інтерфакс-Україна"»). It is still not used as the peak — LB's own correspondent on the scene gave «близько 3 тисяч», a ~3× gap that is flagged, not silently resolved — but it is a reported figure, not the fabrication class of day 1's «кілька тисяч». Aggregators (Telegraf, mignews) also circulated it. Separately, the v3 discovery method was rerun across the whole wave (gazetteer-grep over every downloaded round-up, batch tier-A queries, targeted city searches) and surfaced **no verifiable city beyond the 25**: the four cities a competitor's Telegram map flagged (Irpin, Boryspil, Kalush, Drohobych) appear in no round-up body and no city-specific report, and every gazetteer hit was a false positive — the organiser's surname «Козятинський», the President's name «Володимир», war-attack city lists (Ізюм, Марганець), a media brand («Еспресо.Біла Церква», which the outlet separately reported as *not* protesting), the ammunition-depot case «Вишневе». **A date trap persists:** several pieces search surfaces as «second/third day» are in fact dated a day earlier — read the article's own date before trusting a day label.

**12. The 6 August run (days 5–22): three traps specific to collecting a long wave.** *(a) Articles cite other days' figures.* Assigning every number in a page to the page's own date produced a Kyiv «20 000» on 3 August — a retrospective mention of the 31 July march inside a 3 August report — and a «10 000» on 22 July, which was LIGA recapping 17–18 July. Figures are therefore only accepted when the sentence carries no conflicting date and no retrospective marker («нагадаємо», «раніше», «напередодні», «за час протестів»). *(b) Regional-desk page furniture manufactures protests.* Suspilne's regional pages wrap every article in "related articles" teasers, so a Dnipro report carries the string «Суспільне Івано-Франківськ», and a Kolomyia page carries «До 785-річчя Коломиї» — which parsed as a crowd of 785. Two filters were needed: any sentence repeated across six or more documents is page furniture and is dropped, and the article's own headline must be about the protests. *(c) Numbers that are not crowds.* «розпочалася близько 9:00» became nine people, the photo-gallery counter «1 / 14» became fourteen, and «понад 17 років живе у Дніпрі» became seventeen. *A fourth trap survived into the data and was caught only at verification:* stripping photo credits before storing a quote left 19 quotes mutilated rather than verbatim; they were re-extracted from the unmodified body, and the five that could not be recovered cleanly were demoted to «невідомо» rather than published as approximations.

**13. Day numbering in the sources is not reliable; the calendar date is.** On 28 July UKR.NET called it day 13 while ANTIKOR called it day 11. Dnipro.media numbers its own local actions and reached the 23rd by 6 August, one more than the wave has days, because some days carried two actions. Every row here is anchored to the calendar date of the event, never to an outlet's day label.

**14. Headline inflation, caught again — this time in a wire agency.** Ukrinform's day-3 Uzhhorod piece is headlined «кількість мітингувальників... **зросла до двох сотень**», but the body contains no absolute number at all, only its own correspondent's «нині на зібрання прийшло майже вдвічі більше людей, аніж раніше». Under warning 4 the body governs, so Uzhhorod stays `<100` on its verified counts (police «близько 50» on day 1, «близько 20» on 30 July, «лише 12 людей» on 5 August) and is marked contested.
